01 · The customer problem

04 · What the MVP does

A six-step evidence-linked workflow

The system compares consecutive 10-Ks, checks earlier disclosures, and preserves the research record.

1

Ingest

Load frozen prior and current SEC filings with timestamps and source identity.

2

Compare

Identify added, removed, and materially changed language in matched sections.

3

Classify

Apply fixed categories, direction, materiality, and confidence rules.

4

Check prior disclosure

Search strictly earlier 8-Ks and pre-filing headlines; exclude same-day news.

5

Explain with evidence

Show why the item may matter beside exact prior and current excerpts.

6

Preserve the record

Retain filing links, timestamps, hashes, offsets, and analyst context.

The system answers: What changed? Where? Is it economically meaningful? Was it already disclosed? Why might an analyst care? Where is the exact evidence?

06 · Guided case study

Kraft Heinz: covenant and refinancing language

Selected for evidence diversity and clarity—not future performance.

Current filing2023-02-16Item 1A
Change categoryDebt or refinancing pressure
MaterialityHigh · confidence 0.947
NoveltyPreviously disclosed in 8-K

Prior filing language

Market and benchmark-rate risk

“Changes in financial and capital markets… may increase the cost of financing as well as the risks of refinancing maturing debt… [LIBOR] rates… phase out… by the end of June 2023.”

Current filing language

Default, acceleration, waivers, and facility access

“The creditors who hold our debt could accelerate amounts due… If we breach any covenants… we may not be able to obtain a waiver… and [could be] unable to access our Senior Credit Facility.”
Added

Specific language on acceleration, covenant breaches, waiver dependency, default, and facility access.

Removed

General market-disruption, interest-rate volatility, refinancing, and LIBOR-transition language.

Why an analyst may care

Debt and refinancing changes can alter cash demands, financing access, and downside exposure. The analyst should reconcile covenant headroom and waiver status.

Earlier filing-time evidence

An intervening 8-K and a strictly earlier headline existed. The 10-K adds detail; the underlying issue should not be treated as wholly new.

Ex post—observed later, not available at filing time: the frozen packet reports later return and risk outcomes. They were never used for classification or case selection.

07 · What the research found

A product can work even when the market claim does not.

Negative and null results were preserved rather than tuned away.

A · Raw 10-K feature study

No incremental improvement

Adding 10-Ks did not improve the existing 8-K and cheap-news model.

B · Expected-language study

Persistence baseline won

The prior 10-K alone predicted the next filing’s representation better than models augmented with characteristics, 8-Ks, and news.

C · Disclosure-change demo

Product works; risk test inconclusive

The system reliably identified evidence-backed changes, but the bounded risk test was inconclusive.

Supported

  • Point-in-time integrity
  • Evidence extraction
  • Disclosure comparison
  • Product demonstration

Not supported

  • Proven alpha
  • Causal prediction
  • Validated risk signal

Still to test

  • Repeat use

08 · Next pilot

The next question is operational—not whether another model can rescue an unsupported trading claim.

Quality

Material-event recall; category and materiality agreement.

Trust

Excerpt correction rate; novelty-label acceptance; stored-evidence usefulness.
