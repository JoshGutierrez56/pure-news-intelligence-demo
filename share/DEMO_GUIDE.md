# Demo Guide

## Three-minute walkthrough

1. **Overview:** state the problem—analyst attention is scarce.
2. **Ranked Changes:** open one evidence-linked change and show prior/current excerpts.
3. **Research Results:** explain that the original trading thesis was not supported.
4. **EFX correction:** show how full-prior evidence rejected a plausible story.

Talking point: “The product’s value is not generating a story for every filing. It is preserving evidence and rejecting unsupported stories.”

## Five-minute walkthrough

Follow the three-minute path, then:

5. **RH:** show facts, inference, uncertainty, mechanism, falsification conditions, and no actionable trade view.
6. **Methodology:** show point-in-time discipline and counterevidence retrieval.

Talking point: “Evidence coverage 1.00 means the published packet’s factual propositions are supported—not that its hypothesis is true or predictive.”

## Ten-minute walkthrough

1. Overview and pivot
2. Ranked Changes
3. Contrasting Case Studies
4. RH grounded hypothesis
5. DVN hypothesis with uncertainty
6. EFX safe rejection
7. Four-stage Research Results
8. Methodology and public architecture
9. Known Limitations
10. Pilot Plan

## Likely questions and boundary-preserving answers

**Did this beat the market?**  
No. The original return-prediction thesis was not supported.

**Why call two packets publishable?**  
They passed bounded evidence and skeptic gates as research hypotheses. They were not approved by analysts and did not produce trade views.

**Why is EFX a success?**  
It demonstrates that full-prior retrieval and numeric-role checks can stop a misleading narrative.

**Why only eight cases?**  
V2 was a bounded repair test. Formal human review must precede broader generation.

**What is live?**  
The public interface and frozen packets. No live AI inference runs on GitHub Pages.

**What is next?**  
Blinded human review, then a workflow pilot if the evidence warrants it.
