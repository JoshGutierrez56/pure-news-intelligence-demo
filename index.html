What changed in the project

From prediction to decision support

The locked empirical tests did not support incremental alpha. Customer discovery revealed a stronger need: prioritization, historical comparison, visible evidence, and institutional memory.

The innovation story in 60 seconds

A negative result produced a more defensible product.

Information overload was the customer problem. Evidence-linked review became the value proposition.

1

Attention is scarce

Institutional analysts receive more information than they can review and must reconstruct history manually.

2

The alpha thesis failed

Locked tests did not show incremental value from 10-Ks or enriched expected-language models.

3

Discovery sharpened the need

Six interviews emphasized prioritization, trust, provenance, integration, and human judgment.

4

The project pivoted

The product and quantitative research were separated around a narrow SEC filing workflow.

5

The MVP works

It compares consecutive 10-Ks, checks earlier disclosures, and exposes exact supporting evidence.

6

The next test is a pilot

A controlled analyst workflow study should measure time, recall, trust, corrections, repeat use, and memory value.

Frozen demonstration

Innovation-class framing

One user, one buyer, one testable value proposition

Human judgment remains the final decision point.

Target user

Institutional research analyst

Needs to focus attention, compare history, verify evidence, and explain conclusions.

Buyer and gatekeeper

Head of Research + CTO

Need measurable workflow value, integration, security, governance, and auditability.

Value proposition

Read the right thing first

Understand what changed and preserve the supporting record without weakening analyst judgment.

Disciplined innovation

Evidence changed the project rather than being tuned away.

The research did not prove alpha, but it helped reveal a better innovation opportunity.
