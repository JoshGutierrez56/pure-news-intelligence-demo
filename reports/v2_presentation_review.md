# V2 presentation review

## Narrative test

The ten screens now answer, in order:

1. What problem exists?
2. What do current tools miss?
3. What did six interviews reveal?
4. What was the original hypothesis?
5. What did the tests reject?
6. Why did the project pivot?
7. What does the workflow do?
8. What does one real case look like?
9. What is supported, unsupported, inconclusive, or untested?
10. What pilot and feedback are requested?

## Delivery controls

- Ten-screen standard mode.
- Seven-screen 5-minute mode that skips optional context while retaining the problem, discovery, negative result, pivot, workflow, case, and pilot.
- 15–30 second speaking prompt on every screen.
- Notes hidden by default and toggled with `N`.
- Left/Right, Up/Down, Space, Page Up/Page Down, Home/End, Escape, and `F`.
- Previous/next controls, progress bar, slide number, jump-to-demo, return-to-overview, print view, and clean exit.

## Live-review findings

- The product is visible by screen 7 and the complete case follows on screen 8.
- The strongest headline is the pivot: “The research did not prove alpha. It revealed a better innovation opportunity.”
- The Kraft Heinz example demonstrates why more specific language is not necessarily a first disclosure.
- The final screen requests a decision on the next evidence gate rather than a broad endorsement.
- No slide treats the null result as an apology or hides the `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` classification.

## Projector review

Screens 1, 3, 6, and 8 were manually reviewed at 1920×1080, 1440×900, 1024×768, and 390×844. Desktop hierarchy is legible from a screen share; smaller viewports retain scrollable content and complete keyboard navigation.

## Result

Presentation gate passed for a five- to ten-minute professor walkthrough.
